## 3. The gap

Template kits and AI-generated UI get you to “looks like an app” fast. Users still bounce when it feels empty: wrong hierarchy, limp states, copy that doesn’t match the pixels, motion that distracts instead of guides.

Stakes: trust, retention, and the quiet failure mode — looks done, feels hollow.

The gap isn’t more screens. It’s **judgment in the product** — what to delete, what to emphasize, and how it behaves when things go wrong.

## 8. FAQ

**Design-only or design + build?**  
Either. Some teams want craft and specs for their eng. Others want the same partner through ship. We pick on the first call from your constraints — not a package upsell.

**What does “bespoke” actually mean here?**  
Built for your users and constraints — not a theme with your logo swapped in, and not a concept that can’t be engineered. It does **not** mean endless custom ornament.

**How fast can this move?**  
A focused slice (core flows + a coherent UI system start) can move in weeks when scope is honest. Deep polish across a large surface takes longer. I won’t sell “world-class UX in five days.”

**How does feedback work?**  
Short loops in the product (or prototype), not 40-page PDF comment wars. You own product calls; I push on taste and clarity without ego.

**Startup taste vs enterprise taste?**  
Same craft standard, different constraints. Enterprise tools can still feel human — that’s often the point of this pillar. We don’t paste a consumer skin on a workflow that needs density and speed.

**How is this different from a design agency?**  
Agencies optimize for decks and handoff theater. I optimize for what ships and what users feel. Same factory can also wrangle AI with production controls when the product needs that too.

**What do you need to start?**  
Who it’s for, the job the product must do, hard constraints (brand, stack, compliance), and examples of “feels right” / “feels wrong” — even if they’re competitors.
